# Prediction Market Trading Engine

A Python toolkit that runs the full pipeline of an algorithmic trading desk on
binary event contracts:

1. **Market simulation**: synthetic event markets with a price-time priority limit order book.
2. **Signal research**: a model-implied probability compared with the market-implied one, evaluated as a probability forecast.
3. **Execution and backtesting**: an event-driven backtester with risk limits, a transaction-cost model and two execution styles.
4. **Risk monitoring**: tick-by-tick PnL, Sharpe, drawdown and exposure, a drawdown circuit breaker, and an exportable tear sheet.

> **All market data in this repository is synthetic.** It is generated by
> `market_sim/markets.py` from a fixed random seed. Nothing is scraped from,
> replayed from, or calibrated to any real prediction market. Every number
> this code prints describes a simulated world whose inefficiencies were put
> there by the generator's parameters. **None of it is a claim about
> real-market performance.**

## Quick start

```bash
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements-dev.txt        # numpy + pytest, pytest-cov, ruff, mypy

python main.py                              # full end-to-end run (about 10 s), prints everything
python -m pytest --cov                      # tests + branch coverage (fails under 90%)
ruff check . && ruff format --check .       # lint / format
python -m mypy                              # strict type checking

python -m market_sim.generate_data --seed 42 --out output/synthetic_markets.csv
```

`main.py` options: `--seed`, `--n-markets` (default 50), `--history-markets`
(default 200), `--output-dir` (default `output/`), `--log-every` (monitor
snapshot interval in steps), and `--skip-sensitivity`.

Outputs written to `output/` (git-ignored, and fully reproducible from the seed):

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `synthetic_markets.csv` | every (market, step) row: mid, touch, depth, flow, info features, outcome. Tagged `data_source=synthetic`. |
| `tearsheet_{aggressive,passive}.txt` | the rendered tear sheet |
| `tearsheet_*_equity.csv` | per-step equity, drawdown and gross exposure |
| `tearsheet_*_summary.json` | every tear-sheet metric in machine-readable form |

## Methodology

### Module 1: Synthetic markets and order book (`market_sim/`)

**Latent information and the "true" probability.** Each market trades for
`L` steps (1 step = 1 synthetic hour). A latent information process
`W_i = W_0 + Σ sqrt(v_s)·ξ_s` accumulates Gaussian news. Most steps carry small
variance, and with probability `news_prob` a step is a "news" step with
`news_scale²` times the variance. Variances are normalised to sum to 1. The
market resolves YES iff `W_L > 0`, where the last increment arrives after
trading stops. Because the remaining variance `V_i` is known, the exact
conditional probability is `p_i = Φ(W_i / sqrt(V_i))`. It starts near 0.5
(`W_0` is small) and is pulled toward 0 or 1 as `V_i → 0`. `p_i` is used only
as a diagnostic ("oracle"). No strategy code reads it.

**Market price.** The market's log-odds follow `logit(p_i)` with partial
adjustment (`anchor += κ·(logit p − anchor)`, κ = `market_kappa`), plus an
AR(1) "sentiment" term. The mid is the sigmoid of that sum, clipped to
[0.02, 0.98]. The price therefore **under-reacts to news and carries
mean-reverting noise**. These are the two inefficiencies the strategy can
exploit, and they exist because the generator puts them there.

**Observable features.** `fundamental_z` is a read of `W_i` with persistent
AR(1) error, scaled by remaining uncertainty. `poll_z` is a lagged read of `W`
with independent noise.

**Liquidity.** Each market draws a tier (thin / medium / deep). The tier sets
the median top-of-book depth (40 / 200 / 800 contracts) and the per-level size
decay. Depth wanders over time as a lognormal AR(1). The half spread is
`round(spread_coef / sqrt(depth/100) · (1 + sensitivity·|Δmid|))` ticks, so
spreads are wider in thinner markets and just after moves.

**Order flow.** Per-step taker volume is Poisson, with a rate proportional to
depth and to the size of the price move. It is split into buy and sell flow,
tilted toward the direction of the move *over the same step*: a resting bid is
most likely to be hit while the price is falling (adverse selection).

**Limit order book** (`order_book.py`). Prices are integer ticks from 1 to 99
($0.01 to $0.99). Matching follows price-time priority: the best price fills
first, FIFO within a level, and trades execute at the maker's price. Limit
orders cross first and then rest; market orders walk the book, and any
unfilled remainder is cancelled (partial fills). `insert_at_queue_position` re-inserts an order with a specific
amount of size ahead of it. The passive executor uses it to carry queue
priority across book rebuilds.

**Book construction** (`book_builder.py`). At each step, a two-sided book is
rebuilt around the synthetic mid. The touch sits `half_spread` ticks either
side, and level `k` holds `top_depth · decay^k` contracts (with lognormal
noise). Large orders walk deeper levels, so slippage depends on size relative
to depth.

### Module 2: Signal research and calibration (`signals/`)

* **Train/evaluate split by market, never by time step.** The models are fit
  on a separate **historical universe** of 200 resolved synthetic markets
  (seed offset 10,000). The 50 "live" markets are used only for out-of-sample
  evaluation and the backtest. Resolved outcomes are the only labels, and each
  market contributes one. The first version trained on half of the 50 live
  markets, which gave 25 labels. That overfit: an intercept learned the
  training set's YES/NO imbalance, and momentum coefficients cancelled each
  other out. A desk would calibrate on a long history of resolved markets,
  which is what the historical universe stands in for.
* **Info model** (the "model-implied probability"): L2 logistic regression of
  the outcome on `fundamental_z` and `poll_z`. It never sees the price.
* **Composite model** (the traded signal) is a market-anchored correction:
  `logit q = logit(mid) + b1·(info_logit − logit(mid)) + b2·momentum + b3·mean_reversion + b4·vw_momentum`.
  Market log-odds enter as a fixed offset, so `b = 0` reproduces the market
  price. Neither model fits an intercept, because the features are centred and
  an intercept mostly fits base-rate noise.
* **Features** are strictly causal: momentum (Δ log-odds over 12 steps), mean
  reversion (negative z-score of log-odds against a 24-step mean), and
  volume-weighted momentum. Causality is tested by perturbing future inputs.
* **Regularisation** is chosen by grouped (by-market) 5-fold cross-validation
  on the training markets only.
* **Mispricing / edge** = model probability − market mid. Model probabilities
  are bounded to [0.001, 0.999].
* **Evaluation** (on out-of-sample markets, every 4th step):
  * Brier score and log loss.
  * Murphy decomposition (reliability − resolution + uncertainty).
  * Calibration curve in 10 buckets, and expected calibration error.
  * Information coefficients against the resolved outcome **and** against
    the next-12-step mid change.
  * PnL-if-traded: 1 contract toward the edge, held to resolution, at mid
    and at the touch, both for every signal and for the first signal per
    market.

### Module 3: Execution engine and backtester (`execution/`)

**Event loop.** A heap-ordered queue of `RESOLUTION`, `MARKET_DATA` and
`END_OF_STEP` events. On each `MARKET_DATA` event, the backtester:
1. marks the market to mid;
2. advances any resting passive order;
3. reads the signal;
4. sizes the position;
5. trades against a book rebuilt from the snapshot.

`END_OF_STEP` marks the portfolio and calls the risk monitor. `RESOLUTION`
settles the position at $1 or $0.

**Sizing** (`sizing.py`):
* **Hysteresis:** enter or flip when |edge| ≥ 5¢, hold while |edge| ≥ 1¢,
  otherwise go flat.
* **Fractional Kelly for a binary contract** (×0.25): `(q−c)/(1−c)` long,
  `(c−q)/c` short.
* **Drawdown throttle:** sizing shrinks linearly from 100% at 4% drawdown to
  25% at 10% drawdown.
* **Hard caps:** 400 contracts per market and $4,000 gross worst-case loss.
  Exposure is measured as worst-case loss: `pos·price` for longs and
  `|pos|·(1−price)` for shorts.
* **Order-level limits:**
  * each order is capped at 50% of the visible depth in the top 3 opposite
    levels;
  * minimum trade size is 5 contracts;
  * a 25% rebalance band suppresses small adjustments, except for reductions
    while the book is over its gross limit.

**Transaction costs** (`costs.py`). Every fill is measured against the mid at
decision time and split into three parts:
* **spread:** mid to touch;
* **slippage:** touch to achieved average price, from walking the book;
* **fees:** default $0 per contract, configurable.

A 10-step markout (the fill's value at the mid 10 steps later) measures
adverse selection.

**Market impact.** Walking the book is the temporary cost. In addition, each
aggressive fill shifts the centre of the books the strategy trades against by
2 ticks per unit of top-of-book depth consumed, decaying by 10% per step.
Without this, the book would refill around an unchanged mid every step, and the
same mispricing could be harvested repeatedly at no extra cost.

**Execution styles** (`executors.py`):
* **Aggressive:** marketable orders. Immediate fills that pay the spread plus
  slippage.
* **Passive:** a limit order joins the back of the touch queue, or steps one
  tick inside when the spread is ≥ 3 ticks. Each step:
  1. the order is re-inserted into the rebuilt book with the queue position
     it has earned;
  2. that step's opposing taker flow is sent through the book, and our order
     fills only after the size ahead of it;
  3. if the market trades through the limit, the remainder fills at the
     limit.

  Orders that are not complete after 5 steps are cancelled. At placement, an
  ex-ante Poisson fill probability is recorded, and `main.py` compares it with
  realised fill rates.

### Module 4: Monitoring (`monitoring/`)

* `RiskMonitor.on_tick` runs every step *during* the backtest. It updates:
  * total and rolling (48-step) PnL;
  * rolling Sharpe;
  * drawdown and max drawdown;
  * exposure by market and gross exposure.

  It raises alerts when a rule changes state: a drawdown warning at half the
  breaker level, and an optional exposure warning.
* **Circuit breaker.** When drawdown from the high-water mark reaches 15%, the
  monitor returns `FLATTEN_ALL`, and the backtester:
  1. cancels resting orders;
  2. sends marketable orders to close every position, continuing on later
     steps if a book is too thin to absorb a position at once;
  3. halts new trading.

  The halt lasts for the rest of the run by default; alternatively, it lasts
  for a cooldown, after which the high-water mark is rebased.
* **Tear sheet.** Contents:
  * equity curve and drawdown series;
  * total return, and Sharpe computed hourly (×√8760) and on 24-step "days"
    (×√365);
  * max drawdown and max gross exposure;
  * hit rate (share of traded markets with positive PnL);
  * Brier scores (composite and market);
  * average slippage per fill and cost per contract;
  * cost breakdown by liquidity tier, and markouts.

  It is printed and also exported as `.txt`, `.csv` and `.json`.

### What `main.py` prints

1. **Universe summary.** Per tier: number of markets, spreads, depth and
   volume. Also an order book demo (FIFO priority and a partial fill), and a
   slippage table for market orders of 10 to 500 contracts in each tier.
2. **Signal research.** Selected regularisation and coefficients; Brier, log
   loss, ECE and Murphy decomposition for the market mid, info model,
   composite and oracle; calibration curves; information coefficients; and
   PnL-if-traded.
3. **Aggressive and passive backtests.** Live monitor snapshots every 48 steps
   and a full tear sheet for each style.
4. **Execution-style comparison.** Costs, markouts, PnL, Sharpe and drawdown
   side by side; the realised cost difference per contract; and the passive
   fill-model check.
5. **Circuit-breaker stress test.** The strategy trades the *inverted* signal.
   The output shows the trip step, the positions flattened, the flatten cost,
   and a PASS/FAIL check that all positions are flat and nothing traded
   afterwards.
6. **Sensitivity sweep.** The whole pipeline is re-run while varying analyst
   noise, sentiment noise, market adjustment speed κ and the impact
   coefficient.

## Interpreting the results

These are qualitative readings of the default run (seed 42). The exact
numbers come from `main.py`.

* **Brier score and trading PnL disagree, and the reason matters.**
  Held to resolution, the composite's Brier score is slightly *worse* than the
  market mid's, and its IC against the outcome is close to zero. Outcome noise
  dominates the scores of any forecast of a single resolution.

  Yet both backtests are profitable. The IC against the *next 12 steps'* price
  change is strongly positive. The strategy monetises short-horizon
  convergence: the price catching up with information (κ < 1) and sentiment
  noise mean-reverting. It does not monetise better outcome prediction.
* **The edge is an input, not a finding.** The sensitivity sweep shows PnL
  scaling with the generator's inefficiency parameters. In a fast-adjusting,
  low-noise synthetic market (κ = 0.30, sentiment σ = 0.03), the forward IC
  collapses and the same strategy **loses money** to transaction costs.
  Sharpe ratios in the double digits are a symptom of the synthetic world:
  * stationary, fully specified inefficiencies;
  * no competing traders;
  * books that refill every step;
  * annualisation of a roughly 20-day sample.

  They are not an achievable target.
* **Execution style.** Aggressive execution pays the spread and slippage on
  every fill. Passive execution earns the spread on the fills it gets, but
  fills well under half of what it orders, so it trades far fewer contracts
  and captures less of the edge. `main.py` quantifies the per-contract cost
  gap and each style's net PnL. Costs rise steeply from deep to thin markets.
* **The naive fill model is poorly calibrated.** The ex-ante Poisson
  probabilities are poorly calibrated against realised fills. Some orders fill
  by price trading through them, which the model ignores. Others are
  cancelled early when the signal changes. That gap is reported rather than
  tuned away.

## Known limitations

These are stated plainly because they bound what the output can support.

**The synthetic market model is much simpler than a real prediction market.**
* One Gaussian latent driver per market with *known* remaining variance.
  Real events have fat-tailed, discrete news, ambiguous resolution criteria
  and uncertain resolution timing.
* Markets are independent. There are no correlated or mutually exclusive
  contracts, no cross-market arbitrage, and no common factors such as the same
  candidate across several contracts.
* The inefficiencies (slow adjustment, AR(1) sentiment) are stationary,
  parametric and chosen by the author. Their size directly sets the
  strategy's PnL, as the sensitivity sweep shows. The defaults were also
  chosen while looking at seed-42 output. During development, the market
  adjustment speed (0.12 → 0.08), the sentiment noise (0.10 → 0.12) and the
  analyst-feature noise (0.25 → 0.05 → 0.10) were all changed, so the default
  world has *some* exploitable signal. Treat the default results as a
  demonstration of the machinery, not as evidence.
* The historical calibration universe comes from the *same* data-generating
  process as the live markets. "Out-of-sample" here means new draws, not a
  new regime.

**The order book is a stylised reconstruction, not a model of real liquidity.**
* It is rebuilt every step from a smooth geometric depth profile around the
  synthetic mid. Real prediction-market books are often thin, lumpy,
  one-sided near 0 or 1, and dominated by a few market makers who fade,
  cancel or reprice in response to flow, including ours.
* There is no hidden liquidity, no competing strategies, no queue dynamics
  beyond the strategy's own order, and no latency.
* Taker flow is exogenous, so it does not react to the strategy's quotes.
* Market impact is an ad-hoc linear transient model whose coefficients are
  not calibrated to anything.
* The tick size is fixed at $0.01. Fees default to zero. There is no
  collateral or capital lock-up, no interest on margin, no settlement delay,
  no disputes, no venue position limits, and no withdrawal friction. All of
  these matter on real venues.

**Backtest and statistics.**
* One seed, 50 markets, about 480 synthetic hours. Observations within a
  market are strongly autocorrelated, so per-step statistics overstate the
  effective sample size. Annualised Sharpe ratios extrapolate a very short
  sample.
* Risk limits are enforced at decision time using the current mids. Price
  moves between decisions can push gross exposure above the cap until the
  next decision reduces it.
* The composite model's second stage is fit on the first stage's in-sample
  predictions (simple stacking, no cross-fitting).
* The passive fill-probability model is a simple Poisson queue model, and the
  output shows it is poorly calibrated.
* The circuit breaker flattens with marketable orders. In thin books that
  can take several steps, and it is costly. Both are reported, not hidden.

## Testing and code quality

* `pytest` covers every module:
  * **order book:** fills, partial fills, price-time priority, queue insertion;
  * **calibration math:** Brier, log loss, calibration curve, ECE and Murphy
    decomposition, each checked against hand-computed cases;
  * **feature causality**, and the logistic regression recovering known
    coefficients;
  * **sizing and risk limits;**
  * **cost accounting, and aggressive and passive fill simulation;**
  * **backtester invariants:** PnL fully attributed to settled markets, and
    caps respected;
  * **a forced-drawdown test:** it must trip the breaker, flatten every
    position (including a multi-step flatten in a thin book) and block
    further trading;
  * **the tear sheet;**
  * **end-to-end integration**, including `main.py`.
* Branch coverage is enforced at ≥ 90% (`[tool.coverage.report] fail_under`).
  There are no skipped or xfail tests.
* `ruff` (lint and format) and `mypy --strict` are both clean.
